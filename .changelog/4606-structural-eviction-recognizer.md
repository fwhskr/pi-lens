- **The eviction sweep asks the question structurally instead of matching spellings (#4606).** `tests/config/bounded-eviction-idiom-sweep.test.ts` recognised five lexical spellings of a hand-rolled evict-oldest block, and every review round found a neighbouring shape the previous spelling could not see: a wrapped accessor chain, an alias, an indirect accessor, a binding split across statements, destructured iterator results, an ordering that finishes in its own statement. The question now lives in `tests/support/eviction-programs.ts`, a recognizer over the TypeScript AST built on the S0 census seam, which tests the four stages an eviction program passes through: a cap bound, an order, a victim selection, and an effect on state. It admits four forms (`iterator-head`, `ordered-victim`, `ordered-scan`, `count-truncation`) and refuses everything else; the state table is the module header's and the worktree's `STATE_TABLE.md`. Selection is structural, not a spelling: an iterator's `.next()` head, an array-destructured head, and a `for…of` head that breaks are the same eviction, and a victim reached through a second binding or an alias is resolved before the removal test. The population is 24 flagged programs over 790 census files (`iterator-head 3, ordered-victim 11, ordered-scan 4, count-truncation 6`); eight of them the lexical detector could not see (`clients/sgconfig.ts`, `clients/cache-manager.ts`, `clients/mutation-attribution.ts`, `clients/widget-state.ts`'s retention cap, `clients/lens-map.ts`, `clients/observed-mutation.ts`, `clients/resource-sampler.ts`, `scripts/analyze-pi-lens-logs.mjs`) with retained policy reasons. Three rows that recorded lexical NON-members are deleted rather than re-pinned, because the structural recognizer does not flag a median, a percentile, or a lockfile diagnostic. Six live retention programs the recognizer still refuses are tabled with their own statement anchors and asserted unflagged: insertion-order `push`-then-`slice(-N)` retention in two shapes, an ordered array of edits, a runtime line budget, a minimum reduction under a tracked-count cap, and a runtime-bound suffix retention.
